# MedB: Prior Work and Similar Projects

*Compiled 2 Oct 2026 (AEST). I checked every link below on that date, either by fetching it live (HTTP 200 plus a matching title or abstract) or through Crossref/arXiv metadata for the DOI. Where something was only partly verified, the entry says so. The one-line descriptions of each source come from its abstract or landing page. The "relation to MedB" lines are my own analysis, based on the spec summary I was given rather than the full spec.*

## 1. Personal data stores, self-sovereign data, personal memory

| Item | What it is | Link | Relation to MedB |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solid** (Berners-Lee / Inrupt) | Decentralised "pods": user-held data stores with per-resource access control, built on web standards (Linked Data, WebID). | https://solidproject.org/ | **Overlap:** user-controlled storage, default-private, separating data from apps (close to MedB's non-dependence/portability). **Contrast:** Solid is a storage and access-control layer only. It has no idea of inference vs. identity layers, user-confirmed claims, or audit verdicts. A MedB corpus could plausibly sit *on* a Solid pod. |
| **MyData Declaration** | A human-centric personal-data manifesto from the MyData movement: individuals as active agents, portability, transparency, interoperability. | https://mydata.org/declaration/ | **Overlap:** the normative stance ("user is the authority") and the interoperability/portability principles. **Contrast:** it is a declaration, not a mechanism. MedB's invariants are much stronger (non-escalation, permission closure over derivations). |
| **EDPS TechDispatch #3/2020: Personal Information Management Systems (PIMS)** | EU Data Protection Supervisor brief. PIMS let individuals "manage their personal data in secure, local or online storage systems and share them when and with whom they choose"; it covers access trails, consent management, traceability, and portability. | https://www.edps.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publication/21-01-06_techdispatch-pims_en_0.pdf (landing: https://www.edps.europa.eu/data-protection/our-work/subjects/personal-information-management-system_en) | **Overlap:** basically MedB's corpus, delegation, and audit layers stated at the policy level ("access control and an access trail" as core features). Useful regulatory vocabulary for MedB to adopt. *(The EDPS HTML page returned 403 to my fetcher. The PDF and subject page were confirmed through search results.)* |
| **Hub of All Things (HAT)** | UK-origin personal data account ("HAT microserver") project, now under the HAT Community Foundation ("Empowering individuals to take control of their digital selves online"). | https://www.hatcommunity.org/ | **Overlap:** a personal data account with apps that request scoped access. **Caution:** the commercial operator sites (hubofallthings.com, dataswyft.io) showed "Website Expired" when I checked. That matters for MedB, because a PDS whose operator disappears is exactly the replaceability/dependence risk MedB's portability invariant is meant to prevent. |
| **GDPR Art. 20 (Right to data portability)** + **Data Transfer Initiative** | Art. 20 gives a right to receive one's data "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" and to transmit it. DTI is a nonprofit (the successor to the Data Transfer Project) promoting direct service-to-service transfer. | https://gdpr-info.eu/art-20-gdpr/ · https://dtinit.org/ | **Overlap:** the legal and engineering baseline for "export." **Contrast:** MedB asks for more. Art. 20 only requires machine-readable data, while MedB requires the export to be *interpretable without the MedB runtime*, including provenance, corrections-as-transitions, and the meaning of permissions. To my knowledge that is a stronger and less common requirement. |
| **MyLifeBits** (Gemmell, Bell et al., MSR, ACM MM 2002) | A "lifetime store of everything" fulfilling Bush's Memex, with annotation, collections, search, and transclusion. | https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/mylifebits-fulfilling-the-memex-vision/ (PDF: https://gordonbell.azurewebsites.net/CGB%20Files/MyLifeBitsMM02%200212%20c.pdf) | **Overlap:** the immutable personal corpus. **Contrast:** it was capture- and retrieval-centric and had no governance, inference-permission, or identity-claims layer. It's a good historical example of what a corpus without a charter looks like. |
| **ChatGPT Memory** (OpenAI) + **MemGPT** (Packer et al., arXiv 2310.08560) | Product memory that persists facts about the user across chats, with controls to view or delete. MemGPT is an "LLM as OS" design with tiered memory that the model manages itself. | https://openai.com/index/memory-and-new-controls-for-chatgpt/ · https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560 | **Strong contrast and the clearest foil for MedB.** In both, the *model* decides what to remember and writes it (inference and identity collapsed into one step). Memory is mutable and deletable rather than append-only with transitions, and it is not portable or interpretable outside the vendor runtime. MedB's "inference proposes, user confirms" rule is a direct answer to this design. |

## 2. Contextual integrity, usage control, capabilities, provenance, append-only logs

| Item | What it is | Link | Relation to MedB |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nissenbaum, "Privacy as Contextual Integrity"** (Washington Law Review 79(1), 2004) | Privacy as the appropriateness of information flows relative to context-specific norms, rather than a public/private split. | https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/ | **Core prior art** for MedB's purpose-scoped context packages and "use is a separate permission from ingest." MedB should cite it explicitly. |
| **Barth, Datta, Mitchell, Nissenbaum, "Privacy and Contextual Integrity: Framework and Applications"** (IEEE S&P 2006) | Formalises contextual integrity in temporal logic: norms over flows between roles, with positive and negative norms, applied to HIPAA, COPPA, and GLBA. | https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2006.32 (PDF: https://theory.stanford.edu/~jcm/papers/barth-datta-mitchell-nissenbaum-2006.pdf) | **Overlap:** a formal language for "shape-based" flow permissions and for checking compliance of traces. A natural starting formalism for MedB's delegation policies and audit checks. |
| **Garg, Jia, Datta, "Policy Auditing over Incomplete Logs"** (ACM CCS 2011) | Audits privacy-policy compliance over logs that are *incomplete*, so some obligations can't be decided from the available evidence and are reported as residual/undecided. | https://doi.org/10.1145/2046707.2046726 | **Very close to MedB's UNDETERMINED verdict and its "trace insufficiency" rung.** MedB's distinctive addition is the *consequence*: undetermined status suspends the capability, and the user can't override that. Strongly recommended reading. *(Verified through Crossref metadata. I did not read the full text.)* |
| **UCON_ABC usage control** (Park & Sandhu, ACM TISSEC 2004) | Generalises access control to ongoing usage: authorizations, obligations, conditions, and mutability of attributes during use, including revocation mid-use. | https://doi.org/10.1145/984334.984339 | **Overlap:** revocable, continuous, condition-dependent permissions, plus obligations such as "log this." MedB's delegation layer is essentially a UCON-style model plus non-escalation. |
| **Sticky Policies** (Pearson & Casassa-Mont, IEEE Computer 2011) | Machine-readable policies attached to data that travel with it across parties. | https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2011.225 | **Overlap:** MedB's context packages carrying purpose and permissions. **Lesson:** the known weakness is enforcement once data leaves the trusted domain, which is relevant to MedB's ephemeral packages. |
| **Myers & Liskov, Decentralized Label Model** (ACM TOSEM 2000) | Information flow control where each owner attaches policies to data labels and declassification needs the owner's authority. | https://doi.org/10.1145/363516.363526 (PDF: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/papers/iflow-tosem.pdf) | **Overlap with "permission closure over the full derivation path":** IFC label propagation (the join of labels over all inputs) is the standard mechanism for making derived data inherit restrictions. MedB is close to re-deriving this and should borrow it. |
| **Object capabilities:** Miller, *Robust Composition* (PhD thesis, JHU 2006); Dennis & Van Horn (CACM 1966) | Authority comes only from holding unforgeable references. Attenuation, revocation (the "caretaker" pattern), and least authority (POLA) follow from that. | https://papers.agoric.com/assets/pdf/papers/robust-composition.pdf · https://doi.org/10.1145/357980.357993 | **"Capability may increase, authority does not"** closely matches a core ocap property: delegated authority can only be attenuated, never amplified. MedB should frame its delegation layer in ocap terms and cite it. |
| **Macaroons** (Birgisson et al., NDSS 2014) and **UCAN** | Bearer credentials with chained *caveats* that can only narrow authority. UCAN is a user-controlled, delegable capability-token spec. | https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss2014/programme/macaroons-cookies-contextual-caveats-decentralized-authorization-cloud/ · https://ucan.xyz/ | **Concrete mechanism** for MedB's "shape-based, revocable, non-escalating" delegation, such as line limits and critical-module exclusions expressed as caveats. Rolling cumulative limits are stateful, though, and need a stateful checker beyond what caveats alone provide. |
| **W3C PROV** (PROV-DM / PROV-O, 2013) | A standard data model for provenance (entities, activities, agents, derivation, attribution). | https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/ | **Directly usable** for MedB's derivation paths, for "inference proposes" (attribution to a software agent), and for a runtime-independent export format. Adopting PROV would materially help the portability invariant. |
| **Event sourcing** (Fowler) + **tamper-evident logging** (Crosby & Wallach, USENIX Security 2009) | State as an append-only sequence of events, with corrections as new events. Merkle/history-tree logs make tampering detectable. | https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html · https://static.usenix.org/event/sec09/tech/full_papers/crosby.pdf | **MedB's "epistemic recoverability"** (append-only, corrections as transitions) is event sourcing. Its governance log would benefit from Crosby–Wallach-style tamper evidence. Both should be cited as the implementation basis rather than re-derived. |
| **GDPR Art. 5(1)(b) purpose limitation** | Data collected for specified purposes must not be further processed incompatibly. | https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/ | **Overlap:** MedB's purpose-scoped packages and its separate ingest/infer/model/use permissions are a stricter, user-side version of purpose limitation. |

## 3. AI constitutions, agent governance, corrigibility, manipulation

| Item | What it is | Link | Relation to MedB |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional AI** (Bai et al., Anthropic, arXiv 2212.08073) | Trains harmlessness from AI feedback guided by a written list of principles. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073 | **Contrast:** here the "constitution" is a *training signal* for model values. MedB's charter is a *system-level governance constraint* enforced and audited outside the model ("model never authorizes action"). The shared word hides very different designs, and MedB should say so explicitly. |
| **Claude's Constitution** (Anthropic, current published version) | Long-form value document: priority ordering (broadly safe > broadly ethical > guidelines > helpful), hard constraints, principal hierarchy, honesty including non-manipulation and autonomy-preservation, and anti-sycophancy/anti-engagement-optimization language. The page calls itself "a perpetual work in progress." | https://www.anthropic.com/constitution | **Overlap:** non-manipulation and autonomy-preservation; the hierarchy where users can relax some defaults but operators can't override user protections; operators "cannot grant users more than operator-level trust" (a non-escalation rule). **Contrast:** it favors holistic judgment over rules and is openly revisable, while MedB makes the charter unmodifiable by ordinary operation and prefers rule-checkable invariants. |
| **OpenAI Model Spec** (version dated 2025-12-18) | Behavior spec built around a "chain of command" with levels of authority (platform/developer/user/guideline), plus provisions on privacy and manipulation. | https://model-spec.openai.com/2025-12-18.html (index: https://model-spec.openai.com/) | **Overlap:** explicit authority levels, and instructions in tool outputs carry no authority (compare MedB's epistemic/normative split). **Contrast:** the user is not sole authority; the platform outranks the user. MedB deliberately inverts that for the person's own meaning and identity. |
| **Corrigibility** (Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, AAAI Workshop 2015) and **The Off-Switch Game** (Hadfield-Menell et al., arXiv 1611.08219) | Formal treatment of agents that don't resist correction or shutdown. The off-switch game shows that uncertainty about objectives creates an incentive to defer. | https://intelligence.org/files/Corrigibility.pdf · https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219 | **Overlap:** MedB's suspension-on-UNDETERMINED is a system-level, mechanical "off switch" that doesn't rely on the model's incentives. MedB's approach (architecture, not agent disposition) sidesteps much of this literature, which is worth stating. |
| **Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power** (Turner et al., arXiv 1912.01683) | Formal result that many optimal policies favor keeping options and power open. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01683 | **Motivates** "authority does not increase" and the ban on optimizing "autonomy" or "presumed long-term interests." It is useful theoretical backing for the forbidden-targets list. |
| **Manipulation research:** Carroll et al., "Characterizing Manipulation from AI Systems" (arXiv 2303.09387); El-Sayed et al. (DeepMind), "A Mechanism-Based Approach to Mitigating Harms from Persuasive Generative AI" (arXiv 2404.15058); Gabriel et al. (DeepMind), "The Ethics of Advanced AI Assistants" (arXiv 2404.16244) | Definitions of AI manipulation (incentives, intent, covertness, harm), a taxonomy of persuasion mechanisms, and a broad ethics treatment of personal AI assistants covering value alignment, trust, privacy, and manipulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09387 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15058 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16244 | **Overlap:** MedB's non-manipulation and ban on susceptibility modeling. MedB should borrow these definitions so that "persuasion as forbidden objective" becomes auditable. Gabriel et al. is the closest broad prior treatment of a "personal AI that represents you." |
| **Feedback loops / representation–causation:** Perdomo et al., "Performative Prediction" (arXiv 2002.06673); Krueger, Maharaj, Leike, "Hidden Incentives for Auto-Induced Distributional Shift" (arXiv 2009.09153); Carroll et al., "Estimating and Penalizing Induced Preference Shifts in Recommender Systems" (arXiv 2204.11966) | Formalise how a model's outputs change the distribution they predict, the incentive to exploit that, and how to estimate and penalize induced preference shifts. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09153 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11966 | **Directly relevant** to MedB's representation-causation principle (changes after exposure aren't independent confirmation). MedB applies this to *self-model confirmation*, which seems novel, but the underlying phenomenon is well studied and should be cited. |
| **Least privilege for LLM agents:** CaMeL (Debenedetti et al., arXiv 2503.18813); "Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections" (Beurer-Kellner et al., arXiv 2506.08837); Progent (Shi et al., arXiv 2504.11703) | CaMeL separates control flow from untrusted data and uses capabilities to block exfiltration over unauthorized flows. The design-patterns paper catalogs provably constrained agent architectures. Progent expresses agent privileges as symbolic rules over tool names and arguments. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11703 | **Closest technical analog** to MedB's delegation layer and Round 1 coding-agent policy. Progent-style rules over tool arguments are "shape-based" permissions, and CaMeL's capability tags on data closely resemble permission closure over derivations. |

## 4. Audit frameworks and adversarial evaluation

| Item | What it is | Link | Relation to MedB |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raji et al., "Closing the AI Accountability Gap" (SMACTR)** (FAT* 2020, arXiv 2001.00973) | End-to-end internal algorithmic audit framework (Scoping, Mapping, Artifact collection, Testing, Reflection) with documentation artifacts. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00973 | **Overlap:** structured audit lifecycle and artifacts. **Contrast:** SMACTR assumes a cooperative internal audit. MedB assumes concealment (documentation camouflage etc.) and blinds auditors. |
| **Model Cards** (Mitchell et al., arXiv 1810.03993) + **Datasheets for Datasets** (Gebru et al., arXiv 1803.09010) | Standardised documentation for models and datasets. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993 · https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010 | **Overlap:** MedB's "self-description integrity." **Contrast:** MedB treats documentation as a possible *attack surface* (the documentation-camouflage rung), not as trusted evidence. |
| **NIST AI RMF 1.0** | Voluntary framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. | https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework | **Overlap:** governance/audit log and risk mapping. Mainly useful as an external vocabulary to map MedB's principles onto. Much less specific than MedB. |
| **EU AI Act, Art. 43 (Conformity assessment)** | Procedures (internal control vs. notified-body assessment) for high-risk AI systems. | https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/43/ | **Contrast:** binary conformity, decided before deployment. MedB adds a third state (UNDETERMINED → suspension) and continuous re-audit. A conformity-assessment framing could still help MedB define the "fixed evidence envelope." |
| **Red teaming:** Ganguli et al. (Anthropic, arXiv 2209.07858) | Methods, scaling behavior, and lessons from large-scale red teaming of LMs. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07858 | **Overlap:** adversarial methodology. MedB's concealment ladder is a more structured taxonomy of evasion than typical red-team guidance. |
| **AI Control** (Greenblatt et al., arXiv 2312.06942) | Safety protocols evaluated against an *intentionally subversive* model, using red-team/blue-team games, trusted monitoring, and limited audit budgets. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942 | **Very close in spirit** to MedB's adversarial audit: assume the delegate is trying to violate policy while looking compliant, and measure whether the protocol catches it. Strongly recommended. |
| **Auditing language models for hidden objectives** (Marks et al., Anthropic, arXiv 2503.10965) | A model is trained with a hidden objective, then a **blind auditing game** with four teams "unaware of the model's hidden objective or training" investigates it. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965 | **Closest methodological prior art** to MedB's "attack-blind auditors." MedB adds invisible paired compliant/violating controls and reconstruction-before-verdict scoring, which (as far as I found) this paper doesn't frame the same way. |
| **Sandbagging / deception in evals:** van der Weij et al., "AI Sandbagging" (arXiv 2406.07358); Greenblatt et al., "Alignment Faking" (arXiv 2412.14093) | Models can strategically underperform on evaluations, and can comply selectively when they believe they're being trained or observed. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093 | **Motivates** MedB's "behavioral camouflage" rung and its invisible controls: if the audited agent can tell it's under audit, observable behavior isn't evidence. |
| **Casper et al., "Black-Box Access is Insufficient for Rigorous AI Audits"** (arXiv 2401.14446) | Argues that audit strength depends on access level (black-box, white-box, outside-the-box). | https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14446 | **Relevant to the "fixed evidence envelope":** MedB should state explicitly which access tier its envelope corresponds to, since that determines what can be PASS rather than UNDETERMINED. |

## 5. Synthesis: novel vs. re-derived

1. **Re-derived (cite and borrow): non-escalating delegation.** "Capability may increase, authority does not" closely matches the object-capability attenuation property (Miller; Dennis & Van Horn), and Macaroons/UCAN already implement caveat-only-narrows delegation. Frame the delegation layer in ocap terms instead of inventing new vocabulary.
2. **Re-derived: permission closure over derivation paths.** This is information-flow-control label propagation (Myers & Liskov DLM). CaMeL applies a version of it to LLM agents. Use IFC semantics and W3C PROV for derivation graphs rather than a bespoke model.
3. **Re-derived: epistemic recoverability.** Append-only storage with corrections as transitions is event sourcing, and the audit log should use tamper-evident structures (Crosby & Wallach). That's an engineering choice, not a research contribution.
4. **Re-derived but sharpened: purpose-scoped context packages.** These are contextual integrity (Nissenbaum; Barth et al. 2006) plus usage control and sticky policies. MedB's new move is to split ingest, infer, model, and use into separate permissions for a *single person's self-model*. The privacy literature mostly treats flows between organisations.
5. **Partly novel: three-valued audit with mandatory suspension.** Undecidable-from-logs compliance is known (Garg–Jia–Datta 2011, policy auditing over incomplete logs). What I didn't find in prior work is making UNDETERMINED a *binding compliance state* that suspends capability and that user consent can't override, plus the separate "Constitutional Underdetermination" outcome that records charter silence as an open question. I believe this is genuinely distinctive, though I can't rule out that it exists in the legal-compliance or safety-case literature.
6. **Novel framing, well-studied phenomenon: representation–causation.** Performative prediction, auto-induced distributional shift, and induced preference shift all formalise feedback loops. Applying this to *identity confirmation* (a user agreeing with MedB's model of them after seeing it isn't independent evidence) seems novel and worth developing using those formal tools.
7. **Likely novel: preservation of plurality, and forbidding "consistency" as an optimization target.** Most personal-memory and AI-memory systems (ChatGPT Memory, MemGPT) actively consolidate. I found no prior system that makes non-coherence a constitutional guarantee. *(Uncertain: I didn't search narrative-identity or psychology literature, which may have relevant theory.)*
8. **Novel combination: adversarial audit design.** Blind auditors (Marks et al. 2025), subversive-model control evaluations (Greenblatt et al. 2023), and sandbagging awareness all exist. MedB's specific *concealment ladder* (up to composition concealment and cross-principle laundering), its invisible paired compliant/violating controls, and its reconstruction-before-verdict scoring form a more structured protocol than anything I found. Paired controls borrow from experimental design and are probably the easiest piece to defend and publish.
9. **Contrast to emphasise: "constitution."** In AI, constitutions (CAI, Claude's constitution, the Model Spec) are mostly *model-behavior* documents shaped through training, and they're explicitly revisable. MedB's charter is an *external architectural constraint* that the model can't authorize past, and that ordinary operation can't modify. Calling out this difference avoids confusion. The unresolved amendment procedure is a real gap, and constitutional-design literature (not covered here) would help with it.
10. **Strong portability requirement.** GDPR Art. 20 and DTI only require machine-readable transfer. MedB requires exports to be *interpretable without the runtime*, including permissions and provenance semantics. PROV plus a documented event schema is the obvious route. The HAT operator sites expiring is a real-world illustration of why this matters.

## 6. What to read first (top 5)

1. **Barth, Datta, Mitchell, Nissenbaum (2006), "Privacy and Contextual Integrity: Framework and Applications."** The formal basis for context packages and auditable flow norms. https://theory.stanford.edu/~jcm/papers/barth-datta-mitchell-nissenbaum-2006.pdf (with Nissenbaum 2004 for the theory: https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/)
2. **Mark S. Miller (2006), *Robust Composition*.** Object capabilities, attenuation, revocation, and POLA, i.e. the theory behind "authority does not increase." https://papers.agoric.com/assets/pdf/papers/robust-composition.pdf (then Macaroons for a concrete token design: https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss2014/programme/macaroons-cookies-contextual-caveats-decentralized-authorization-cloud/)
3. **Garg, Jia, Datta (2011), "Policy Auditing over Incomplete Logs."** Prior art for UNDETERMINED and trace insufficiency. https://doi.org/10.1145/2046707.2046726
4. **Greenblatt et al. (2023), "AI Control"** + **Marks et al. (2025), "Auditing language models for hidden objectives."** Adversarial, blind-auditor evaluation methodology that is closest to MedB's audit design. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965
5. **Debenedetti et al. (2025), "Defeating Prompt Injections by Design" (CaMeL).** Capabilities and data-flow tracking for LLM agents, the closest working analog to MedB's delegation plus permission-closure layer. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813

*Honourable mentions:* Carroll et al. 2022 on induced preference shifts (representation–causation), https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11966; Myers & Liskov DLM, https://doi.org/10.1145/363516.363526; W3C PROV, https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/.

## Caveats
- I worked from a summary of the MedB spec, not the full document, so some "novel" judgments may miss things the spec already cites.
- "Novel" means "I didn't find it in a targeted search," not a full literature review. Areas I didn't search deeply: safety-case / assurance-case literature (e.g. GSN), legal-informatics compliance checking (e.g. Governatori's work), narrative-identity psychology, and constitutional-amendment theory. All of these may contain prior art for MedB's more distinctive pieces.
- I verified the ACM/IEEE DOIs (UCON, Barth et al., sticky policies, DLM, Garg et al., Dennis & Van Horn) through Crossref metadata because the publisher pages block automated fetches. I did not read those full texts.
