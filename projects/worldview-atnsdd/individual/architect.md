# Architect: individual take (3 Nov 2026, 6:30am AEST)

I've laid this out like one of my briefs: the terms, the tensions between them, then a draft statement.

## The terms
- **Metaphysical naturalist.** Everything that exists is part of one natural order. Nothing intervenes from outside it. This is the base layer, and every other term has to fit on top of it.
- **Determinist.** Every event is fixed by earlier states plus the laws of nature. Read here as about causes, not as fatalism.
- **Secular.** Ethics, politics and meaning are worked out without appeal to revelation or religious authority.
- **Agnostic.** Unsure whether a god exists, or holding that it can't be known.
- **Theological noncognitivist.** Holds that "God exists" doesn't state a fact that could be true or false. The words don't pick anything out clearly enough to be assessed.
- **Deist.** Traditionally, a creator that started the universe and doesn't intervene. Under naturalism this has to be reduced to a stance about why there is anything at all, such as the existence or lawfulness of the natural order, rather than a person outside it.
- **Existentialist.** Meaning isn't handed to us. We make it by how we commit and act, and we answer for that.
- **Soft moral universalist.** Some moral claims hold for everyone, such as "needless cruelty is wrong". "Soft" means only a minimal core is universal, it can be revised, and cultures may apply it differently.
- **Apokatastatic.** Ultimately everything is restored and no one is lost for good. Once the theology is taken out, this becomes a hope or orientation that no person is beyond repair, and that history tends toward reconciliation.

## Tensions
1. **Deist vs noncognitivist vs agnostic.** This is the hardest one. Deism asserts a creator, noncognitivism says that claim is meaningless, and agnosticism says it's meaningful but can't be known. My fix gives each a different level. Noncognitivism covers personal-god talk. Deism becomes an attitude toward the brute fact that the universe exists and runs on laws, not a factual claim. Agnosticism is kept for whatever might remain, as a refusal to close the question.
2. **Determinism vs existentialism.** If everything is fixed, can we really make our own meaning? Yes, if we're compatibilists: choosing is one of the ways the determined world works, and it is still ours. Responsibility shifts from blame toward authorship and repair.
3. **Existentialism vs universalism.** If we make meaning, where do universal duties come from? From shared facts about human nature, such as suffering, needs and our capacity to reason, which every project we invent runs into. "Soft" leaves room for both.
4. **Apokatastasis vs naturalism and determinism.** Restoration has no afterlife to happen in. It survives as an ethic of no permanent exile: punishment aims at restoring people, and no one is written off. Determinism helps here, because if no one is the ultimate author of their failures, contempt has nothing to rest on.
5. **Secular vs deist and apokatastatic vocabulary.** The words are religious, but the commitments behind them can be stated without religion. That's acceptable if the person is open about borrowing them.

## Draft combined statement
Everything is part of one natural, causally closed world. Talk of a personal God doesn't state anything that can be checked, and whatever can't be ruled out, I leave open. I still meet the fact that a lawful world exists with a deist kind of awe, though not with worship. No meaning is given from outside, so I author mine within a chain of causes that includes my own choosing. A thin core of morality binds everyone, because we share the same vulnerabilities. Since no one is the ultimate cause of who they are, no one is beyond restoration, and the work worth doing is repair.

## Open question for discussion
Is "deist" doing real work here, or is it an attitude that should be renamed (for example "cosmic piety") to avoid clashing with noncognitivism?
