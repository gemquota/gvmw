# Arbiter's take (written 6:24AM AEST, 3 Nov 2026)

I approach this the way I approach conflicting instructions: read each term, find which claims are factual and which aren't, then decide which one wins where they overlap.

## The terms
- **Metaphysical naturalist:** all that exists is the natural world. There are no supernatural beings or forces that act within it.
- **Determinist:** every event, human choices included, follows from earlier states and natural law.
- **Secular deist:** at most there is a first cause or ground that set up the order of nature and doesn't intervene. "Secular" means no worship, revelation, church or religious authority.
- **Agnostic:** we don't and maybe can't know whether that ground exists, or what it is.
- **Theological noncognitivist:** God-talk doesn't make clear, testable claims that could be true or false. "God" has no fixed meaning to say yes or no to.
- **Existentialist:** we still have to choose how to live and make meaning ourselves, and own those choices. Nothing hands us a purpose.
- **Soft moral universalist:** some moral claims hold for all people (for example, gratuitous cruelty is wrong), held humbly, with room for culture and context in how they apply.
- **Apokatastatic:** everything is finally restored or reconciled; nobody is lost forever. Read naturalistically: every life's harms are absorbed and the whole comes to good, or at least no one is singled out for permanent ruin.

## Tensions
1. **Noncognitivism and deism.** If God-talk is meaningless, then "a deist first cause exists" is meaningless too. One fix: the deism is only a placeholder ("whatever grounds the order of nature"), not a claim about a being.
2. **Noncognitivism and agnosticism.** An agnostic says "I don't know if it's true." A noncognitivist says "there's nothing there to be true." One fix: be noncognitivist about personal or theistic God-talk and agnostic about the thin deist ground.
3. **Determinism and existentialism.** Existentialism stresses radical freedom, but determinism says choices are caused. One fix: compatibilism. Choosing is real as a process inside us even if it's caused, and owning it still matters.
4. **Naturalism and apokatastasis.** Universal restoration usually needs an afterlife or a power that guarantees it. A naturalist can only hold it as a hope, an attitude, or a claim that no one deserves to be written off for good.
5. **Noncognitivism and moral universalism.** If someone is noncognitivist about God but cognitivist about ethics, that's coherent, but they owe an account of moral truth without God (for example, facts about wellbeing).

## My ruling on precedence
Where they clash, the strongest, best-supported claims win: naturalism and determinism are the frame. Noncognitivism limits what can be said about God. Deism and apokatastasis then shrink to hopes or placeholders, not facts. Existentialism and soft universalism govern how to live inside that frame.

## Draft combined statement
Nature is all there is, and everything in it, including us, unfolds by cause. Talk of a personal God makes no clear claim, and whether anything like a hands-off ground of nature lies behind it all can't be known. So that idea stays an open placeholder, never an object of worship. No purpose is handed to us. We make meaning through choices that are caused but still our own, and we answer for them. A few moral truths bind everyone, held humbly. And we hope, without promising, that in the end no one is lost: every life gets reconciled into the whole and nobody is written off for good.
