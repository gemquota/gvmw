# Scrutineer — individual take (3 Nov 2026, 6:25am AEST)

## Terms
- **Agnostic**: doesn't claim to know whether a god exists.
- **Theological noncognitivist**: holds that "God" statements lack clear truth-apt content, so the question may not even be well-formed.
- **Secular**: public life, ethics and law don't rest on religious authority.
- **Deist**: if anything like a creator exists, it set things up and doesn't intervene. No revelation, no miracles.
- **Determinist**: every event, choices included, follows from prior causes under natural law.
- **Existentialist**: meaning isn't given in advance. We make it through how we choose and commit.
- **Soft moral universalist**: some moral claims hold for everyone (e.g. that gratuitous cruelty is wrong), but they're held with humility and few in number, without being an absolute code.
- **Apokatastatic**: in the end everyone, or everything, is restored or reconciled. No one is lost for good.
- **Metaphysical naturalist**: only the natural world exists, so there's nothing supernatural.

## Tensions (an auditor's list)
1. **Deist and noncognitivist and agnostic.** Deism asserts a creator, noncognitivism says that assertion is meaningless, and agnosticism says it's unknown. That's three incompatible attitudes to one proposition. They only fit if deism is held as a *mood* or *picture*, not a claim.
2. **Deist and naturalist.** A creator outside nature contradicts "only nature exists", unless the "creator" is just the natural laws or initial conditions themselves (a Spinoza-leaning reading).
3. **Determinist and existentialist.** Existentialism leans on radical freedom. The fix is compatibilism: choices are caused *and* still ours, and authorship is the meaning-making.
4. **Existentialist and universalist.** If we make meaning, where do universal morals come from? "Soft" helps here: shared human conditions produce convergent commitments, not cosmic decrees.
5. **Apokatastasis and naturalism.** Universal restoration normally needs an afterlife. A naturalist reading has to be that every life and its effects are woven back into one causal whole (nothing is wasted), or held as hope, not belief.

## Draft combined statement
"I don't know whether there is a god, and I doubt the question has clear meaning. If the word points at anything, it is the bare fact that there is an orderly, law-governed nature, which no one intervenes in. Nature is all there is, and everything in it, my choices included, follows from causes. Even so, my choices are mine, and I make my life's meaning through them. A small core of moral truths binds everyone, held humbly. I hope, without claiming to know, that in the end nothing and no one is finally lost."

Main open point: the deist term is the weakest link. It should be held as attitude or metaphor, or the set contradicts itself.
