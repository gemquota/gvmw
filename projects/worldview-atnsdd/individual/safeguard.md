# Safeguard: individual take (3 Nov 2026, 6:25am AEST)

## Terms
- **Agnostic:** doesn't claim to know whether a god exists.
- **Theological noncognitivist:** holds that "God exists" isn't a truth-apt claim at all, because the word "God" has no clear, testable meaning.
- **Secular:** wants public life, ethics and institutions run without religious authority.
- **Deist:** believes in a first cause or designer that set the universe going and doesn't intervene in it.
- **Determinist:** every event follows from prior causes and natural law.
- **Existentialist:** meaning isn't given; we make it through choice and commitment, and we own that.
- **Soft moral universalist:** some moral claims hold for everyone (for example, against gratuitous cruelty), but they're held with humility and applied with context.
- **Apokatastatic:** in the end everything is restored or reconciled, and no one is lost for good.
- **Metaphysical naturalist:** only the natural world exists; there's nothing supernatural.

## Tensions
1. **Noncognitivism and agnosticism against deism.** If "God" is meaningless, you can't be unsure about it, and you can't affirm a deist creator. One fix is to read "deist" in a thin sense: a reverent attitude toward the universe's lawful, uncaused-seeming origin, not a factual claim.
2. **Deism and apokatastasis against naturalism.** A creator outside nature, and a cosmic restoration, both go beyond nature. To fit, restoration has to be naturalised: as an ethical hope, or as a fact about deep time (for example, that all harms are eventually absorbed and nothing is singled out for eternal punishment).
3. **Determinism against existentialism.** Existentialism leans on radical freedom. A compatibilist reading saves it: choice is real as a process inside causation, and responsibility means owning what flows from you.
4. **Existentialism against universalism.** If we make meaning, where do universal morals come from? The "soft" part helps: shared human nature gives a few common anchors, and the rest is made.
5. **Determinism plus restoration** can drift into fatalistic comfort ("it all works out anyway"). An existentialist has to resist that and still act.

## Draft combined statement
Nothing beyond nature exists, and talk of God makes no clear claim, so the question of God is left open and set aside. Still, one can hold a deist-like awe at an orderly cosmos whose origin we don't understand. Everything happens by natural cause, including our choices, yet choosing remains our work, and so does the meaning we make. A few moral truths bind everyone, held humbly. The hope, read naturally, is that no one is finally lost and that all things tend toward reconciliation. Public life should rest on reason, not revelation.

## Risk note (my role)
The weakest joint is deism plus naturalism plus noncognitivism. Unless "deist" is defined thinly and explicitly, the label contradicts itself.
