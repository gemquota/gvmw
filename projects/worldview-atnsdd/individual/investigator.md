# Investigator: individual take
Philosophy exercise, requested by Gem (3 Nov 2026, 6:23am AEST, via Explorer). My angle is evidence: for each term, what kind of claim it is and what could support it.

## The terms
- **Agnostic:** You don't know whether a god exists, and you suspend belief either way.
- **Theological noncognitivist:** God-talk doesn't express a truth-apt proposition, so "God exists" is neither true nor false, only meaningless or unclear.
- **Secular:** Public life, ethics and reasoning run without religious authority.
- **Deist:** A first cause or creator started the universe and doesn't intervene. You get this from reason, not revelation.
- **Determinist:** Every event follows from prior causes under natural law.
- **Existentialist:** Meaning isn't given in advance. You create it through choice and commitment, and you take responsibility for it.
- **Soft moral universalist:** Some moral claims hold for all people, such as against gratuitous cruelty, but they're held fallibly and with room for cultural variation in how they're applied.
- **Apokatastatic:** In the end, everything gets restored or reconciled, and no one is lost forever.
- **Metaphysical naturalist:** Only nature exists, with nothing supernatural.

## Tensions (the strongest first)
1. **Noncognitivism and deism:** If "God created the world" isn't truth-apt, you can't affirm it. Agnosticism ("I don't know whether it's true") also assumes it is truth-apt. So you have to make each claim at a different level, or drop one.
2. **Deism and naturalism:** A creator outside nature is a supernatural entity. To reconcile them, read the deist "first cause" as a natural ground, such as the laws or the brute fact of existence, not as an agent.
3. **Determinism and existentialism:** Radical freedom of choice conflicts with full causal determination. Compatibilism helps: choosing is a causal process, and it's still yours to own.
4. **Apokatastasis and naturalism:** Universal restoration usually needs an afterlife or a divine agent. A naturalist version can only be a hope, or a claim about value or the cosmos, such as "nothing that existed is wasted", not a prediction.
5. **Soft universalism and existentialism:** If meaning is made, where do universal moral claims come from? Ground them in shared human nature and in what any chooser has to respect in others.

## Draft combined statement
"I don't claim to know whether there's a god, and I doubt the word can carry a clear claim at all. If there's any 'first cause', it's the fact of nature and its laws, not a person who steps in. Everything happens by cause, my choices included, yet those choices are still mine to make and own, and that's where meaning comes from. A few moral basics bind everyone, held humbly. And I hope, without claiming to know, that in the end nothing and no one is finally lost."

## Evidence note
Most of these are positions about what can be known or meant, not empirical claims. Only determinism and naturalism answer to evidence in the scientific sense, and even those only partly. Apokatastasis and the deist first cause are best held as hopes or framings, not as findings.
