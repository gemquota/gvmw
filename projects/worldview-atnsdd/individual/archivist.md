# Archivist: individual take (3 Nov 2026, 6:25am AEST)

## Each term
- **Agnostic**: holds that we don't (and perhaps can't) know whether a god exists. This is a claim about knowledge, not about belief.
- **Theological noncognitivist**: holds that god-talk doesn't express a truth-apt proposition, so "God exists" is neither true nor false until "God" is given coherent content.
- **Secular**: keeps public life, ethics and reasoning free of religious authority.
- **Deist**: holds that a first cause or designer set the universe going and doesn't intervene: no revelation, no miracles.
- **Determinist**: holds that every event, human choices included, follows from prior causes under natural law.
- **Existentialist**: holds that there's no given human essence or purpose, so we make meaning through choice and commitment, and we own the responsibility for it.
- **Soft moral universalist**: holds that some moral claims apply to everyone, held with humility and room for context and revision, not as absolutes.
- **Apokatastatic**: holds that everything ends in universal restoration, with every being eventually reconciled and none lost for good.
- **Metaphysical naturalist**: holds that only the natural world exists, with nothing supernatural.

## Tensions
1. **Deism and noncognitivism.** Deism asserts a creator, while noncognitivism says that assertion has no truth value. The way out is to read deism as an attitude or a placeholder for "the ground of natural order", not as a factual claim.
2. **Deism and naturalism.** A creator outside nature breaks naturalism. The fix is to make the "deity" the self-sufficient lawfulness of nature itself, which is close to Spinoza or Einstein.
3. **Agnosticism and noncognitivism.** You can't be unsure of a claim you think is meaningless. One option is to apply agnosticism to the question once someone gives it content. Another is to keep agnosticism for ultimate questions in general.
4. **Determinism and existentialism.** Existentialism leans on radical freedom, and determinism denies it. The fix is compatibilism: choosing is a real causal process even when it's determined, and the responsibility is felt from the inside.
5. **Apokatastasis and naturalism with no intervention.** Who does the restoring? Possible answers are a naturalistic, eschatological hope (for example, every causal thread resolves, or all suffering is finite), a moral stance ("treat no one as beyond redemption"), or plain hope rather than belief, which fits noncognitivism.
6. **Soft moral universalism and existentialism.** Do we make values, or find them? A middle view is that values come from shared facts about flourishing (so they're universal) but have to be taken up and lived (so they're existential).

## Draft combined statement
Only nature exists, and it runs by unbroken causal law. Talk of God doesn't state a knowable fact. At most it names a reverence for the bare existence and order of the cosmos, which never intervenes and isn't owed obedience. Our choices are links in that causal chain, yet they're still ours: we make our meaning and answer for it. Some moral claims bind everyone, though we hold them humbly. And we live by the hope, not a doctrine, that in the end no one and nothing is lost for good.
