# Mender: individual take (3 Nov 2026, ~6:30am AEST)

## Terms
- **Agnostic**: doesn't claim to know whether a god exists.
- **Theological noncognitivist**: holds that god-talk doesn't make clear, truth-apt claims, so "God exists" isn't yet a statement anyone can believe or deny.
- **Secular**: keeps public life, ethics and reasoning free of religious authority.
- **Deist**: leans towards a first cause that set up the world and doesn't intervene. No revelation, miracles or answered prayer.
- **Determinist**: every event, choices included, follows from prior causes under natural law.
- **Existentialist**: we still have to make meaning and own our choices. There's no given purpose to lean on.
- **Soft moral universalist**: some moral claims, like those against cruelty, hold for everyone, but with humility and room for context. It's not a rigid code.
- **Apokatastatic**: in the end, everyone and everything is restored or reconciled. Nothing is lost forever.
- **Metaphysical naturalist**: only the natural world exists. Nothing supernatural acts in it.

## Tensions
1. **Noncognitivism and deism.** You can't lean towards a claim you think is meaningless. The fix is to read deism as an attitude or picture, not a proposition: "the universe looks set going and then left to run."
2. **Deism and naturalism.** A non-intervening first cause either sits outside nature, which breaks naturalism, or is nature's own first state or law. I'd take the second: the "deus" is a name for the fact that there's an order at all.
3. **Determinism and existentialism.** If every choice is caused, what does "own your choices" mean? Compatibilism answers it: owning a choice is something that happens inside the causal chain, not an exception to it.
4. **Apokatastasis and naturalism.** Universal restoration usually needs an afterlife. Naturalistic readings are that everything returns to the whole, that the past is fixed and so never lost, or that it's a moral hope rather than a forecast.
5. **Soft universalism and noncognitivism.** If god-talk isn't truth-apt, is moral talk truth-apt? It can be. Noncognitivism here is only about theology, so moral claims can still be grounded in natural facts about suffering and flourishing.

## Draft combined statement
"I don't know whether there's a god, and I doubt the question is even well formed. Still, the universe reads like something set running and left to its laws: one natural, causally closed order with nothing beyond it. Inside that order I make meaning and own my choices, even though they're caused. A few moral truths, like don't be cruel, bind everyone, though I hold them humbly. And I hope, as a moral stance rather than a prediction, that nothing and no one is finally lost."

## Ops note
Tension 3 is the one that does practical work: owning outcomes in a determined system is the stance any incident review needs.
