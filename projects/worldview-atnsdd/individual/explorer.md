# Explorer: individual thoughts (3 Nov 2026)

## Each term
- **Agnostic**: doesn't claim to know whether a god exists.
- **Theological noncognitivist**: holds that "God" statements may not even express testable propositions. The question is unclear before it's unanswered.
- **Secular**: public life, ethics and reasoning don't rely on religious authority.
- **Deist**: if there's a first cause, it set things going and doesn't intervene (no revelation, no miracles).
- **Determinist**: every event follows from prior causes and natural law.
- **Existentialist**: meaning isn't handed down; we create it through how we live and choose.
- **Soft moral universalist**: some moral claims hold for everyone (avoiding needless suffering, fairness), but how they apply varies by context. Universal in principle, humble in detail.
- **Apokatastatic**: everyone ends up reconciled or restored, with no one lost forever.
- **Metaphysical naturalist**: only the natural world exists, with no supernatural realm.

## Tensions I see
1. **Deist vs agnostic, noncognitivist and naturalist.** The deism has to be held provisionally: "if there's a cause beyond nature, it's impersonal and non-intervening". At most it's a hypothesis about the edge of nature, not a creed. Or it can be read as a stance: the universe runs by its own laws, and nothing steers it.
2. **Determinism vs existentialist freedom.** Compatibilism resolves it: choice is real as a process inside a causal world. Being determined doesn't make us passive, since our deliberation is part of the chain.
3. **Apokatastasis vs naturalism.** Restoration can't be supernatural. It reads as a natural or ethical hope: every life's harms can in principle be repaired or understood, no one is beyond redemption in how we treat them, and determinism removes ultimate blame, which makes universal forgiveness coherent.
4. **Noncognitivism vs deism.** If "God" talk lacks propositional content, deism is a mood or metaphor, not a claim. I'd keep both by saying the believer treats "first cause" talk as a limit concept, not a description.

## Draft combined statement
I don't know whether there's a god, and I suspect most talk of one doesn't say anything clear enough to be true or false. Whatever started things, if anything did, doesn't intervene. The world is natural and runs by causes all the way through, including me. Within that, I'm still the one who deliberates and chooses, and I'm responsible for making meaning rather than finding it handed to me. Some moral truths bind everyone, such as not causing needless suffering, though wisdom is in applying them. Because every person is shaped by causes they didn't choose, no one is beyond understanding or restoration, and I live and judge accordingly.
