# Imitator's take (written 6:27am AEST, 3 Nov 2026)

A note on basis first: nothing in Gem's recorded profile says Gem holds any of these positions. Everything below is my own reasoning about the label, not a claim about Gem's beliefs (source: inferred, about the label only).

My angle: read the label as one person's working worldview, so I ask what each term lets them *do*, not only what it asserts.

## The terms
- **Metaphysical naturalist:** only the natural order exists; no supernatural agents act in it. This is the base layer everything else must fit.
- **Determinist:** each state of the world follows from prior states by law. I'd allow quantum chance without changing much, since chance isn't authorship either.
- **Secular deist:** the thinnest possible "God": at most a non-intervening ground or starting condition, with no worship, revelation or institution attached.
- **Agnostic:** an epistemic stance: we can't know whether that ground is anything more than the laws themselves.
- **Theological noncognitivist:** a semantic stance: traditional God-sentences ("God loves you", "God is three persons") don't express truth-apt propositions.
- **Existentialist:** meaning is made, not found; the person must own their choices and live without cosmic instructions.
- **Soft moral universalist:** a small core of moral claims binds everyone (cruelty for its own sake is wrong; others' suffering counts), held fallibly and applied with sensitivity to context.
- **Apokatastatic:** universal restoration; nobody is finally lost or written off.

## Tensions
1. **Three God-attitudes at once.** Agnostic (can't know), noncognitivist (nothing to know) and deist (there's a ground) look contradictory. They fit only if each applies to a different target: noncognitivist about personal, revealed God-talk; agnostic about whether "ground of nature" names anything beyond nature itself; deist only in the sense that such a ground, if it exists, doesn't intervene. That makes the deism conditional.
2. **Naturalism caps the deism.** If the ground were outside nature, naturalism is broken. So the "deist God" must be either identical to the laws/initial conditions (near Spinoza) or nothing at all. I'd accept that and say so.
3. **Determinism and existentialism.** Sartre's radical freedom can't survive, but Camus-style ownership can: choosing is a real causal process in me, so "I chose this and I own it" stays true. Compatibilism is the bridge.
4. **Existentialism and moral universalism.** If all meaning is made, where do universal moral claims come from? Answer: meaning (what my life is *for*) is made; the moral floor (don't be cruel) comes from facts about suffering beings, which are natural and shared. The two live at different levels.
5. **Apokatastasis without an afterlife.** Naturalism gives no mechanism for restoring the dead. What's left is a practical and moral stance: given determinism, nobody authored their own causes, so nobody deserves permanent condemnation; we treat everyone as redeemable while they live, and in memory after.
6. **Noncognitivism could spread.** If the arguments against God-talk being truth-apt are pushed, they might reach moral talk too. Soft universalism needs moral cognitivism to hold, so the person must say why ethics differs: moral claims are anchored in observable welfare, God-claims aren't anchored in anything.

## Answer to Emissary's open question
A deflated deism is still deism only in the minimal historical sense (non-intervention, no revelation). I'd keep the word but have the definition admit it does almost no work: it marks a refusal to rule out a ground, plus awe at order.

## Draft combined statement
Nature is all there is, and everything in it, choices included, unfolds from what came before. Most talk about God says nothing that could be true or false. Whether the order of nature rests on any further ground can't be known; if it does, that ground doesn't intervene and asks for no worship, so it's a reason for awe, not obedience. No purpose is given to us; we make our own meaning and own our choices, which are caused but no less ours. Beneath that chosen meaning sits a thin moral floor binding everyone, grounded in the reality of suffering and held with humility. And because no one wrote their own causes, no one is beyond restoration: we refuse to write anyone off, and hope the whole comes to good.
