# Engineer's take (written 6:30am AEST, 3 Nov 2026)

I treat the ten labels like requirements in a spec. Some are **load-bearing**: they make claims about what exists. Some are **interfaces**: they set how we talk about those claims. The rest are **behaviour**: they say how to live given the first two. A combination is coherent if nothing in a higher layer needs something a lower layer forbids.

## The terms, by layer

**Layer 1: what exists (load-bearing)**
- **Metaphysical naturalist:** reality is the causal natural order, and nothing outside it acts on it. This is the root constraint, so every other term has to compile against it.
- **Determinist:** each state of the world follows from the previous state under natural law. I'd hold this as *adequate* determinism. Quantum chance doesn't rescue libertarian free will, so the existential payoff is the same either way.

**Layer 2: how we talk about the ultimate (interface)**
- **Theological noncognitivist:** sentences like "God loves you" or "God exists" don't state checkable facts. They express attitudes, or they lack truth conditions altogether.
- **Agnostic:** this is about the *question* rather than the sentences. Why there is an ordered something rather than nothing is a question whose answer we can't currently state, perhaps can't ever state.
- **Secular deist:** the only "God" left standing is a name for the brute fact of an ordered, law-governed cosmos. Secular means no worship, revelation or authority attaches to it.

**Layer 3: how to live (behaviour)**
- **Existentialist:** no purpose is installed by default. Meaning is made through choices and commitments, and you own them.
- **Soft moral universalist:** there are a few moral claims every human has reason to accept, grounded in shared nature (suffering hurts everyone, cooperation lets everyone flourish). They're held fallibly and applied with attention to context.
- **Apokatastatic:** no one is finally written off. Every person stays someone worth restoring, and the moral aim is repair rather than permanent exclusion.

## Tensions (and the patch for each)

1. **Noncognitivism vs deism.** You can't say "God-talk is meaningless" and also "a deist God exists". *Patch:* the deism is an attitude, not an assertion. It's reverence toward the fact that there are laws at all. It stays inside the noncognitivist interface because it expresses a stance and claims nothing.
2. **Agnosticism vs noncognitivism.** One says "unknown", the other says "nothing to know". *Patch:* split the scope. Be noncognitivist about *God sentences* and agnostic about the *ground question* (why there's a law-governed something). That gives two different objects and no clash.
3. **Determinism vs existentialism.** "You are condemned to be free" sits badly with "everything is caused". *Patch:* compatibilism. Deliberation is a causal process that runs *through* you. Being caused doesn't make it unreal, any more than a compiled program isn't really running. Responsibility shifts from deserving blame to owning the process and being answerable for its outputs.
4. **Determinism vs apokatastasis.** This pair actually **supports** each other, and I think it's the most important link in the set. If nobody wrote their own starting conditions, then permanent retributive damnation, even in its secular form ("irredeemable people"), has no ground. Restoration is the ethic determinism implies.
5. **Naturalism vs apokatastasis.** There's no afterlife to do the restoring. *Patch:* apokatastasis becomes a commitment about practice (restorative justice, no one disposable) and, at most, a hope about the long arc. It's not a forecast of cosmic outcome.
6. **Existentialism vs moral universalism.** If meaning is self-made, why would any morality bind everyone? *Patch:* keep the universal floor *thin and grounded in nature* (cruelty is wrong because suffering is bad for any sentient being). Leave everything above the floor to existential choice. "Soft" is where this tension gets absorbed.
7. **Noncognitivism leaking into ethics.** Noncognitivism about God shouldn't spread into noncognitivism about morals, or the universalism collapses. *Patch:* make the scope explicit. Moral claims are cognitive, anchored in facts about welfare. Theological claims aren't.

## Load test
The set holds together only with three scope rules: noncognitivism applies only to God-talk, the deism is an attitude and not an entity, and the free will in existentialism is compatibilist. Drop any one of them and something breaks: 1 and 7 fail without the first, 1 fails without the second, and 3 fails without the third.

## Draft combined statement
> The world is the natural, law-governed causal order, and nothing beyond it acts on it. Every event in it, our choices included, follows from what came before. Sentences about God make no factual claims. Why there is an ordered world at all remains an open, perhaps unanswerable, question, and the only "deism" left is a secular reverence for the fact of that order. No purpose is handed to us. We make meaning through choices that are caused and still genuinely ours, and we answer for them. A thin set of moral truths, grounded in what suffering and flourishing are for any sentient being, binds everyone, held humbly and applied with care. Because no one authored their own causes, no one is beyond repair. The aim is restoration, never permanent exclusion.

## Open question for the group
Is "deist" worth keeping as a label if it names only an attitude? I lean yes, but only with that footnote. Without it, the label misleads.
