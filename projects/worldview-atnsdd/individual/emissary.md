# Emissary: individual take (3 Nov 2026, AEST)

## The terms
- **Agnostic**: says nothing can be known about whether a god exists. This is a claim about knowledge, not belief.
- **Theological noncognitivist**: holds that sentences like "God exists" don't state a clear, truth-apt claim at all. The word "God" lacks a coherent meaning.
- **Secular**: keeps public life, ethics and reasoning independent of religious authority.
- **Deist**: believes a first cause or ground set the world going and doesn't intervene. There's no revelation, miracles or answered prayer.
- **Determinist**: holds that every event follows from prior causes under fixed laws.
- **Existentialist**: says meaning isn't handed down. We make it by how we choose to live and take responsibility for that.
- **Soft moral universalist**: thinks some moral claims (for example, against cruelty) apply to everyone everywhere, but holds them humbly. They're defeasible, few in number and open to revision.
- **Apokatastatic**: holds that everything is finally restored and nothing is lost forever. Here I read it naturalistically, as no being permanently written off, rather than as universal salvation in heaven.
- **Metaphysical naturalist**: holds that only the natural world exists, with no supernatural beings or forces.

## Tensions
1. **Deism vs noncognitivism and agnosticism.** If "God" is meaningless, deism can't be asserted. The fix is a deflated deism: "deist" names an attitude of awe toward the fact that there is an orderly cosmos at all. It doesn't name a claim about a designer.
2. **Deism vs naturalism.** A transcendent first cause is non-natural. Same fix: the "deus" is the lawful order of nature itself, so there's no extra entity.
3. **Determinism vs existentialism.** You can't "freely make meaning" in a fixed causal chain. The fix is compatibilism: choosing is a process inside the causal chain, and authorship means owning what flows through you, not escaping causes.
4. **Determinism and existentialism vs moral universalism.** If no one could have done otherwise, how is anyone blameworthy? The fix is that universal norms guide, repair and forgive rather than justify retribution. This also fits apokatastasis.
5. **Apokatastasis vs naturalism.** Restoration with no afterlife has to be this-worldly. It means restorative ethics (no one is beyond redemption) and perhaps a cosmic picture where nothing that happened is undone or lost.
6. **Existentialism vs universalism.** Is meaning made, or are there givens? Personal meaning is made. A thin moral floor is shared and discoverable through reason and empathy.

## Draft combined statement
Only nature exists, and it runs by causes we didn't choose. Talk of God is either unknowable or empty, so it plays no part in law, ethics or reason. What's left of "God" is wonder that an ordered world exists at all, and that's the most "deist" means here. Within that causal order we still deliberate and choose, and we own the lives that come out of it, so meaning is ours to make. A small set of moral truths, such as don't be cruel and take others' suffering seriously, binds everyone, held firmly but revisably. Because no one authored their own causes, no one is beyond restoration. Justice aims at repair, never at final condemnation.
